7 Ways to Reduce Your Digital Footprint in 2026

Updated 2026 7 min read

Every account you create, every post you make, and every service you sign up for adds another piece to your public digital footprint. Here's a practical checklist for shrinking it.

1. Audit yourself first

Before you can reduce anything, you need to know what's already exposed. Run your own email and usernames through an OSINT tool like SHADOWTRACE to see what's publicly discoverable.

2. Stop reusing usernames

A single unique username reused across every platform makes it trivial to connect your entire online presence. Use different handles for unrelated accounts.

3. Use email aliases

Unique email aliases per service (or a dedicated alias provider) prevent a single breached email from linking all your accounts together.

4. Lock down old, unused accounts

Forgotten accounts on services you no longer use are often the weakest link. Delete or deactivate what you don't need.

5. Review social media privacy settings

Default settings on most platforms favor visibility. Manually review who can see your posts, friends list, and past activity.

6. Check breach databases regularly

New breaches surface constantly. Re-run exposure checks on your key emails every few months rather than once and forgetting about it.

7. Enable two-factor authentication everywhere

Even if your credentials leak, 2FA is often the difference between a minor inconvenience and a full account takeover.
